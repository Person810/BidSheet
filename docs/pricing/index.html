Common questions

Is my data still on my computer if I subscribe?

Yes, always. Cloud sync is a copy, not a move. The app reads and writes your local database first; sync uploads copies so your other computers (and a future fresh install) can pull them down. Cancel, go offline, or delete your account, and the local app and your local data keep working untouched.

Can you read my backups?

No. Everything you sync — jobs, takeoffs, catalog, and plans — plus your whole-database backup is encrypted on your computer before it's uploaded. We store only sealed ciphertext and can never read it.

The encryption is unlocked by a single recovery key, shown to you once, that is not your login password. Save it somewhere safe: it's the only way to unlock your data on a new computer, and because we never see it, we genuinely can't recover your data for you if you lose it.

What does "Beta" mean here?

Cloud sync is newer than the estimating app and still earning trust. It's tested, live, and backed up, but expect rough edges, and expect us to be honest about them. The local app does not depend on it in any way.

What happens when I cancel?

Your subscription runs to the end of the period you paid for, then uploads stop. Your local data is unaffected; the app keeps working in full. Your synced data stays available for download for a wind-down period after lapse so you can pull everything to your machines, after which it may be deleted from our servers. Details are in the terms of service.

How does the free trial work?

Creating a cloud account starts a 30-day trial with 10 GB of storage, with no payment details required. If you subscribe, storage grows to 100 GB. If you don't, uploads stop at day 30; your local data is untouched and anything synced remains downloadable during the wind-down period.

I need more than 100 GB.

100 GB is roughly tens of thousands of compressed plan pages and photos, and most shops never get near it. If you do, contact us and we'll sort out a larger tier.

Can I self-host the cloud?

The desktop app is GPLv3, and pointing it at your own infrastructure is possible if you're technical. It's unsupported, but we won't stand in your way. The hosted service exists for everyone who would rather pay $20 than run servers.
